## The frameworks, verbatim enough to reuse

### Post 3: Kallaway, the Triple Hook (Context → Lean → Contrarian Snapback)
1. **Context, immediately.** Viewers only watch content they have a vested interest in, so the first line says what
   the video is about. "Say it, show it, write it": audio, visuals and on-screen text, ideally all three aligned.
   His claim: "even delaying context to the back half of the first sentence dramatically increases the skip rate"
   ("we have data"; not shown).
2. **The lean.** Make the viewer curious in one direction, so they lean in rather than opt out. Three ways: shock,
   a pain point, or proof (a stat validating the coming claim).
3. **The contrarian snapback.** Pull the viewer away from the direction they were leaning, "like setting a hook in
   a fish".
4. **Self-test:** read the first one or two sentences alone; is the context clear, and is there something that
   makes you lean in?

### Post 12: Kallaway, the Story Ladder (6 levels)
| Level | Name | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reporter | Core facts in a predictable order ("this happened, then this") |
| 2 | Illusionist | Hooks in the intro AND the body: misdirection, leaning one way and snapping back |
| 3 | Champion | Stakes: use cases, problems or characters that make the viewer feel personally connected |
| 4 | Architect | Arcs (tension added and released, like waves) and loops (mini subplots that open and close) |
| 5 | Translator | Clarity and comprehension: reframing, better visuals, polish, so the viewer is clear 100% of the time |
| 6 | Maestro | A signature style, the unique fingerprint |

### Post 8: 20 Reel formats and the 4×4 rule (swastik.nandakumar)
**The rule:** under 5,000 followers, test 4 formats with 4 Reels each. Pick the winner by views, non-follower reach,
and shares plus saves; keep testing new formats on the side. "One video = a coin flip. Four videos = data." His
result: 500 → 33.2K followers in 45 days (self-reported).

**The formats, with their template lines:**
1. Talking head: "Here's why ___ isn't working."
2. Listicle: "X things I wish I knew before ___."
3. Myth vs fact: "Everyone says ___. Here's the truth."
4. Do this, not that: "Stop ___. Do ___ instead."
5. Hot take: "Unpopular opinion: ___."
6. Green screen: "Everyone's talking about ___. Here's what's missing."
7. Screen recording: "How to ___ in under ___ minutes."
8. Voiceover + B-roll: "This is how I ___ every day."
9. Text-only Reel: "Nobody tells you this about ___."
10. Storytime: "How I went from ___ to ___ in ___."
11. 30-day experiment: "I tried ___ for 30 days."
12. Before / after: "My ___ before vs after ___."
13. POV: "POV: you just ___."
14. Day in the life: "A day in my life as a ___."
15. Mistakes: "Stop doing this if you want ___."
16. Tier list: "Ranking every ___ from worst to best."
17. Reply to a comment: "Someone asked: ___? Here's my answer."
18. Remix a creator: "___ is right, but here's what's missing."
19. Breakdown: "Why this ___ got ___ views."
20. Steal my ___: "Steal my ___ for ___."

### Post 9: weak vs viral hooks (dnityasingh), across seven niches
- **Weak:** too generic (anyone could say it), no urgency, unclear audience, low emotional pull.
- **Viral:** hyper-specific (a real person with a real problem), emotion-triggering, one clear promise, aimed at the
  ideal viewer.

| Niche | Weak | Viral |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate | "Tips for buying a house" | "The hidden cost of buying a home that surprises almost every first-time buyer" |
| Skincare | "Tips for glowing skin" | "The skincare mistake that secretly made my acne worse for 2 years" |
| Bakery | "We bake fresh cookies every day" | "Why people drive 40 minutes just to buy these cookies before we sell out" |
| Brand strategist | "How to build your brand" | "Why people forget your brand 3 seconds after seeing your content" |

The other three niches (travel, mindset, aesthetician) follow the same pattern: a general topic becomes one
specific, surprising problem.

### Post 11: six storytelling techniques (marketingwithgaurav), "Hook. Hold. Land."
- **Hook:** a bold statement, a surprising result, a relatable struggle, or a question they can't ignore.
- **Emotional triggers:** frustration, hope, pride, fear of missing out.
- **Open loops:** "open the loop early, always close it".
- **Curiosity gaps:** what they think vs what's true, what they want vs what it takes; always deliver the answer.
- **Pattern interrupts:** an unexpected visual, a shift in tone, a contrarian take, a twist.
- **Pacing:** short sentences, well-placed pauses, cut every extra word, save the best for the right moment.

### Post 13: cookin.code shows vyralcontent/content-skills diagnosing a flopped Reel
- **On screen:** skip rate 71% against a typical 48%; average watch 3.1 s.
- **The diagnosis:** a bad hook (-81% in the first 3 s), a mid-video drop, and reach held back for unoriginal
  content.
- **The fixes:** "cut the intro, open on the result", "add a re-hook at 0:07", "post only original edits for 30
  days", then a trial Reel.

## Tools from batch 2 that touch our writers
- **humanizer** (blader/humanizer, MIT, 69k stars). Read in full through the GitHub API (no download): 498 lines,
  26 patterns, Markdown only; its `package.json` has no scripts.
  - **Adopted (16 Nov):** the phrases a news writer can trip over were added to the blog's banned list
    (`fma/write.HYPE_WORDS`, which the writer is told and the gate checks) and to the Reel cards' style check
    (`fma/reel_desk.TELLS`): "plays a key/crucial/pivotal role", "a pivotal moment", "evolving landscape", "sets
    the stage for", "at its core", "the real question is", "let's dive in", "here's what you need to know", and the
    model disclaimers ("based on available information", "as of my last…", "while specific details are limited",
    "experts argue").
  - **Checked against history first:** none of these appeared in any published post or Reel card.
  - **Deliberately NOT added:** words that do appear in real, fine posts: "highlighting", "showcasing",
    "underscoring", "reflects a broader". Blocking them would have failed good posts.
  - **Not installed as a skill:** our checks are code, and the pipeline must not depend on Claude.
- **last30days** (mvanhorn/last30days-skill, MIT): a trend scan across Reddit, X, YouTube, HN and arXiv.
  - **WATCH for the blog's story collector,** free sources only (Reddit, arXiv, Techmeme, HN). Several of its sources
    need paid keys, and ScrapeCreators is a scraping API we won't use.
  - Not adopted now: `fma/collect` already reads 40+ sources directly.
- **Perplexity, Firecrawl, Apify:**
  - Perplexity: not needed (the pipeline cites its own sources).
  - Firecrawl: AGPL, public pages only if ever needed.
  - **Apify: AVOID** for social scraping.
