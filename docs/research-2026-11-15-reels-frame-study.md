## Shared by all six
1. **Text on frame 0 that states the payoff.** None opens with a greeting or a logo.
2. **A number inside the first 4 seconds.**
3. **The thing of value is shown, not described:** cards, repo pages, a terminal, dashboards, a map, thumbnails.
4. **More on screen than can be read in the time,** which drives pauses, saves and comments.
5. **One low-effort call to action, after the value:** screenshot, or comment one word.
6. **Understandable with the sound off,** at least at headline level.

## Reel 1's cheat sheet, exact text
- **Formats (21):**
  - talking head (long and short form);
  - talking head, walking shot;
  - short B-roll (text hook + caption);
  - long B-roll montage;
  - talking head with changing frames;
  - talking head doing something while speaking;
  - reaction (green screen or split screen);
  - whiteboard;
  - podcast hooks;
  - podcast-style shoot;
  - rating Reels;
  - this or that;
  - problem vs solution;
  - myth or fact;
  - true or false;
  - showing the desktop screen and explaining;
  - Q&A;
  - humour;
  - skits / character play;
  - split screen;
  - tutorial.
- **Tonality:** serious (expert), concerned (you care), loud and excited (grabs attention), casual (authentic), angry (to motivate).
- **Three elements:** information, emotion, entertainment.
- **Hook types:** text, verbal, visual.
- **Verbal hooks:** relationship, celebrity, fear/desire, shock, storytelling, fact-based.
- **Content pillars:**
  - personal;
  - educational;
  - transformation / lead gen;
  - trend jacking;
  - entertainment;
  - authority (bold opinions, predictions, debunking common advice, breakdowns, myths).
- **Attention triggers:**
  - EMOTION (make me feel something);
  - CURIOSITY (information gap, open loop, suspense);
  - RELEVANCE ("this is about me": identity, pain, profession);
  - NOVELTY (surprise, contradiction, pattern interrupt);
  - VALUE ("this will help me": money, time, frameworks);
  - SOCIAL PSYCHOLOGY (status, social proof, FOMO, shareability).

## Reel 5's posting system
One idea becomes 20+ Reels in five placements:
| # | Placement | Goal | What changes | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Trial Reels | Validate what works | 80% of the focus on the hook; also body and call to action | up to 6 a day |
| 02 | Main feed | Publish what worked | Judged on skip rate, leads, views | up to 1 a day |
| 03 | Trial Reels again | Exploit what worked | Better hook, better call to action, re-edit | up to 30 a week |
| 04 | Main feed again | Exploit it on the feed | Re-edit, a call to action in the caption, a new hook | the best of the 30 |
| 05 | Main feed, 30 days later | Maximise leads | Minimal variations | — |
His claim "so Instagram doesn't recognise it" is platform-gaming and unproven. A re-run for us must be a new cut.

## Not to copy (judgement)
- A silent, uncaptioned call to action at the end (Reel 2).
- A 3-second ident (Reel 3).
- Spoken counts that contradict the screen (Reel 1: says "top 5", lists 6).
- A guilt-trip follow ask and "get paid tools for free" framing (Reel 4).
- Re-uploading the same file to slip past Instagram (Reel 5).
- A call to action that lives only in the post caption, or promising DMs that then don't arrive: a visible
  complaint on Reel 6 says "Still not received".
