## 1. Hooks and the first seconds
- **Window:** 2-3 s on short form, sometimes 1-2 (Caleb) [O].
- **Types that work:**
  - a commonly held belief declared false, with the alternative;
  - a pattern interrupt;
  - a thought-provoking question.
  - "Unexpected fact" hooks are wearing out (Caleb) [O].
- **Hooks must not sound like hooks** (Dominik) [E: one comparison, the conversational version got 20,000 likes].
  Viewers have a shield against loud or clever openers; what works sounds like a friend mid-sentence ("did you know
  if you add…"). He and his clients spend 85% of their time on the hook.
- **The governing test:** does this work for a stranger who has never seen us? Instagram tests each post on a small
  group of non-followers and watches swipe-away, time stayed, and whether and when they like (Dominik) [E: his
  reading of analytics]. If 70-80% of views come from followers, the content is made for people who already know you.
- **Open on a desire or a pain**, "if you want X" / "if you don't want Y", never on how-to detail (Dan Koe) [O].

## 2. The idea comes first
- **Ten parts of a video** (Kallaway): topic, angle, format, hook, substance, call to action, story structure,
  visual layout, visuals, audio. Only topic + angle can give 100x for a little more effort; a winning idea survives
  almost any edit, and a losing one is not rescued by perfect execution [O, his experience].
- **How to make a contrarian angle:** list the viewer's common beliefs, write why each is not true and what is true
  instead (Kallaway) [O]. Order of work: expertise, contrarian insights, idea and angle, formats, hooks, the rest.
- **Idea filter:** only ideas that are both likely to perform and exciting to make (Dan Koe) [O].
- **Idea bank** (Dominik) [O]:
  - search Instagram for the three broadest phrases the audience would type and note what gets outsized views;
  - repeat on TikTok and YouTube and feed their suggested phrases back;
  - log topic, hook and patterns; ask what is missing; write 20 one-line ideas.
- **Gap theory** (Dominik) [O]: map the niche in three tiers (100K+, 50-100K, small but rising), record topics,
  formats and hook types, do not copy hooks, write down where you disagree. A grid that looks like four other
  accounts loses the visitor.
- **Formats:** topic from your niche, opinion from you, format from another niche. Search a broad unrelated keyword
  and take the structure, not the content [E: one example Reel with 24.7M views]. Templates: "X things I'd never do
  as a Y"; "four non-negotiables", each line shaped "if you do X, you get Y" where Y ties to the main need.

## 3. Story structure
- **Hook, Problem, Journey, Lesson, Call to action** (Caleb) [O].
  - Problem: specific, relatable stakes.
  - Journey: progress, low points and uncertainty, not a highlight reel.
  - Lesson: usable at once.
  - Call to action: not a sale; ask for the biggest takeaway.
- After each section, list the objections a sceptical viewer would raise and answer them inside the content.
- **Teach through a new lens** and persuade the non-interested: social audiences are not searching (Dan Koe) [O].

## 4. Carousels (Caleb, observed Feb 2025) [E: his observation]
- Slides 1 AND 2 are both hooks: Instagram re-serves the carousel to people who ignored it, starting on slide 2.
- One simple image plus one or two sentences per slide. Listicles work best.
- Two slides per section of the source; for a dense piece, expand one section rather than compressing the whole.
- A photograph of a physical or hand-drawn object also performs.

## 5. Profile, grid and highlights (Dominik) [E: clients "doubled" visitor-to-follower conversion in about two months; no numbers]
- **Two-second test:** a visitor decides from the bio, the highlights and the first posts.
- **Two failures:**
  - obvious selling ("DM me", booking links, highlights called prices or testimonials);
  - random elements (a viral but off-topic pin, a vague cover).
- **Bio:** cover the name; if the bio could sit on anyone's profile it is a slogan. Formula: one line naming a
  specific audience, a specific problem and a specific objection removed, then a free useful resource.
- **Highlights:** leftmost serve the viewer (the core problem, quick results); the rightmost are for action.
- **The first six posts are a sequence:**
  - Pin 1 answers "is this for me?" (the audience's biggest problem) and leads into a next post.
  - Pin 2 proves we are worth following with something only we have (our opinion, our data).
  - Pin 3 brings them in with a free resource tied to the core problem, delivered by comment keyword.
  - Posts 4-6 are reordered by hand for the current need (reach: best follower-converting posts; trust: a story;
    leads: more free-resource entry points).
- **First nine posts:** each must visibly relate to the one main need, for a visitor with no context.
- **Follow window:** follows and purchases are highest in the first 14-30 days after discovery and fall steeply
  after; winning someone takes about 20 Reels consumed, 5-6 of them in the first session [E: his tracking, no figures].

## 6. Measuring (Caleb unless noted)
- **Multiplier sheet** [O, method; "directionally correct, not scientific"]:
  - per platform and per format, the MEDIAN of the last 75-90 days is the benchmark;
  - every post is a multiple of it, scored at day 11 (posts can surge around day 7);
  - the benchmark is refreshed monthly;
  - a 3x post on one platform gets tested on another.
- **Top 10% monthly:** find the one or two things present in ALL the top posts, replicate them, never study the bottom.
- **"Brick by brick" list:** specific things observed to improve outcomes; it becomes the playbook the makers read.
- **Dominik's lists, kept separately:** most views, most followers (usually different posts), deep comments rather
  than emoji, and the flops for what not to do. Judge patterns, never single Reels.
- **Two to four metrics;** if a number would not change what you do, do not track it. About 90% of output backed by
  data, 10% new ideas. Analytics has exactly one owner: when two roles were both asked to analyse metrics, neither did.
- **Accept 30-40% fewer views for better conversion.** Four videos at 250,000 views are not one at a million: the
  small ones drew 60-70% of views from existing followers, the big one mostly new people [E: a client's channel].

## 7. Cadence and experiments
- **Accordion:** volume early to learn (ten posts in a week teach more than ten in a month), then compress the
  effort into fewer, better pieces (Caleb) [E: team anecdote].
- **Dominik:** 3-4 Reels a week tied to the core need; 95% of his clients do not post enough; batch-script 3-5 at a time.
- **70/20/10:** 70% proven, 20% small iterations (hook, call to action, a new format on the same subject), 10% pure
  experiment (Caleb) [O].
- **Content hackathon,** monthly or quarterly: a day making one or two pieces unlike daily work, judged on
  innovation, not performance; the winner is published [E: several long-running top formats came from these].
- **Repurposing 2.0:** let performance on one platform choose what gets remade natively elsewhere, keeping the
  proven hook; never post the same thing everywhere at once [E: a top tweet became a short, a photographed poster,
  a LinkedIn post and a long video, each a top performer].

## 8. Comments and community (Caleb)
- Reply to the commenters you already have: visible replies make new people comment. Reply personally, or from a
  clearly labelled team account.
- Do not run open "what should I make" polls; mine the questions in DMs and comments. If five people ask the same
  thing in a few hours, publish the answer at once, without saying "many of you asked" [E: Gary Vaynerchuk's practice].
- Let the audience choose between two versions. Make the audience the hero by publishing their wins.
- Inside jokes and shared vocabulary. Three pillars: educational, conversational, social proof.
- Calls to action that are not a sale: an emoji if it was useful; a debate on a counter-norm take.

## 9. Trust, positioning, money
- **Trust matrix** (Dan Koe): Growth, Authenticity, Authority. People follow people who share ideas; the levers are
  time under attention (long-form makes short posts land), shared values, and real polarisation [O].
- **Anomaly study** (Dan Koe) [O, method]:
  - 5-10 creators admired for how they phrase things;
  - one hour a day for a week collecting their posts with 2x their usual engagement, 20-30 of them;
  - under each, 3-5 bullets on the pattern, how it took attention, the value, and why people care.
- **Brand** (Caleb): an intentional pairing of relevant things, done consistently. Decide what we will NOT be
  associated with. Start narrow; 80% core topic, 20% "interest stacking".
- **Short form builds awareness; long form converts.** Prioritise two or three platforms, one in focus at a time.
- **1x1x1** (Kallaway): one traffic source, one customer, one offer; two of each is eight times the complexity.
- **Money:**
  - a personal brand is a traffic source, not a business (Dan Koe);
  - first product in a week at about $10, and if about 2.5% convert, build it out;
  - a free resource by comment keyword once or twice a week captures email, and sales happen by email
    (Dominik) [E: self-reported];
  - "share the knowledge, sell the execution" (Caleb).

## 10. The Team section, as rules for an automated pipeline (Caleb)
- **Hire on the constraint:** a stage exists because a named bottleneck exists. Ask where work is queueing (ideas,
  scripts, renders, posting, analysis) and put effort there.
- **"Four Rs" for every role:** Role, Responsibilities, Requirements, Results (a numeric output). For us: every
  pipeline stage has a purpose, owned tasks, input requirements and a measurable output.
- **The tryout:** every candidate gets the identical source and the results are compared side by side, scored on
  the work, on meeting the brief and deadline, and on showing why. For us: every model, prompt or template change
  is tested on the same fixed stories, and a second test asks it to innovate on the format, not only reproduce it.
- **Platform fit:** for Reels, a maker who understands opening and closing loops; the red flag is caring about
  production value without understanding the hook. The audience defines quality, not the team.
- **Start lean:** one end-to-end process first, documented step by step, split into specialists only when volume
  justifies it.
- **Brand consistency is messaging:** a list of topics never discussed, statements that are fine in context but
  harmful when clipped, and who gets shown as an example. An outside maker needs the most oversight.
- **30/60/90 for anything new:** internal only, then published and reviewed, then on its own, with a review at a
  fixed date judged on the trend against the usual.
- **Checklists are quality control, but a team that only follows them stops innovating.** Each maker must be able
  to say why ("it felt right" is not an answer). A weekly teaching session, rotating presenter.
- **Reviewer rule:** spend as much comment on what to keep as on what to change, so the maker does not drift away
  from its strengths [E: an agency reported better performance and retention a year later].
- **Protect making time:** batch reviews into fixed windows.
