[S4] TechCrunch — "ChatGPT’s new Wallet lets AI agents shop and check out for you" (13 November 2026)
ChatGPT can now shop for you. On Friday, OpenAI launched a new wallet feature that lets its agents, called dots , help you decide what to buy and then complete the purchase on your behalf. The option will initially be available to a limited number of U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers, ages 18 and up, with a U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard, or American Express.
OpenAI says that users are fully in control of the purchases, as they’re able to review the items, the total, and the payment card that will be used to complete the transaction before the checkout occurs. In addition, users will approve the purchases directly with a security check like Face ID or a fingerprint, which creates a record of their transaction.
The addition comes as consumer-facing AI tools are working to find the right product-market fit, in terms of how they can help people be more efficient as they complete various tasks. While companies like DoorDash and Uber have so far reported low numbers of orders placed by AI agents, Meta’s Muse, which now has over 10 million users, has seen AI-assisted shopping become a top activity on its app.
Other personal AI agents, like Instinct, also support making purchases on users’ behalf.
It’s no surprise, then, that OpenAI would want to enter this space more with an agentic shopping feature of its own.
To get started, ChatGPT subscribers add their eligible payment cards to the new ChatGPT Wallet, then ask their dot to help with a purchase. (This setup is a one-time thing; users don’t have to re-enter their payment information every time they buy.) When it’s time to transact, ChatGPT’s Wallet charges the selected card using a payment token, which is a substitute for the user’s actual card details, so that information isn’t shared with either the dot or the merchant.
The merchant will automatically receive what it needs to fulfill the order from the dot, like the shipping address and contact information, OpenAI says. The company notes that customers who shop via their dots will still be able to earn their credit card rewards and be able to take advantage of protections that typically come with cards, like being able to dispute transactions.
The limited availability at launch will allow OpenAI to test and improve the feature and its capabilities before rolling it out more broadly. In the future, the company plans to offer users more control over the feature by allowing them to set spending limits or configure other parameters around purchase categories or time periods, so they can pre-approve purchases that their agent can then handle more autonomously.

After a widespread outage shut down Microsoft 365 services for businesses, governments, and tens of millions of other users around the world, the essential cloud services are now (mostly) back online, according to the company’s status page .
The outage debacle began around lunchtime on the East Coast on Thursday, with users reporting Outlook outages to the service-tracking website Downdetector just after 12 pm ET. Within the hour, Microsoft said it was investigating reports of an outage that at the time was limited to North America. It quickly spread to impact more than 70 countries around the world.
The full cause of Microsoft’s unprecedented outage remains unknown, but the company repeatedly ruled out a cyberattack as the cause in statements to its status page. It is also ruling out AI or AI agents playing a role in the debacle.
“We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services,” a Microsoft spokesperson says in a statement. “This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change. We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions.”
The company has promised a “full post-incident report” after it has fully reviewed what went wrong.
As Outlook, Teams, Copilot, and other Microsoft 365 systems come back online, users will likely see their inboxes turn into a mess. “You're probably going to get a lot of notifications when things come back,” says Jonathan Leitschuh, an independent researcher.
Microsoft said it had increased the queue time for emails to 48 hours in an attempt to ensure that every message that people tried to send while its systems were down gets delivered. Leitschuh points out that, even with the bulk of systems restored, users may receive “a flood of notifications a few hours down the pike” as subsystems come back online.
The total impact of the Microsoft outage may never be fully known, but it’s safe to say it’s already significant.
