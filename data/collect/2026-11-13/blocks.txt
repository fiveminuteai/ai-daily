[S6] TechCrunch — "Muse can now place calls to US businesses — if you pay" (12 November 2026)
After initially testing the feature in September, Meta’s consumer AI agent Muse is now able to place calls to U.S. businesses on behalf of its users. However, only paid subscribers have access to the feature, the company says.
Meta’s Muse, which now has over 10 million users , the company said last month, has been working to make consumer AI a mainstream product for everyday users. Placing calls is one of the ways it aims to get there, as it could handle another task on people’s to-do lists.
Notes Alexandr Wang, Meta’s chief AI officer and head of its Superintelligence Labs division, the feature could be used to take care of calls people don’t want to make, like those to their cable company, the DMV, or to a restaurant that doesn’t offer online reservations.
Previously, only a select number of Muse early adopters were able to try this feature after asking the AI for access. Now, anyone who pays for Muse can make this type of request. Currently, Muse subscription plans start at $20 per month for 500 million Muse tokens per week. Heavier AI users can opt to pay $100 per month for 3 billion tokens.
Muse is not the only AI agent able to place calls to businesses; the startup Instinct has been offering a similar option , as has Google’s Gemini . However, while the AI tools are now certainly capable of handling the task, the reception on the other end of the line can be mixed. Many businesses and receptionists hang up on calls from AI agents as soon as they identify that it’s not a human on the phone. But with AI now better able to mimic a real human voice, being able to tell the difference between a customer and an AI working on the customer’s behalf will get tougher.
Despite the challenges, this feature is one that could appeal to consumers who have grown frustrated with the state of customer service over the years. For too long, customers have been forced to navigate complex phone trees, and then sit on hold waiting for a human representative to pick up. If they’re lucky, that person can actually help them, and not transfer them to another department. Now, those consumers could potentially use their AI, with its infinite patience, for this tedious task instead.
While calls remain a paid-only feature for the time being, the recently added option to make a Muse email address will soon roll out to free users, Wang said this week.

[S8] Ars Technica — "Taiwan’s chipmaking giant TSMC to invest $1B in Anthropic, OpenAI" (11 November 2026)
Top chip manufacturer TSMC is ready to invest $1 billion in Anthropic and OpenAI. That would give the Taiwanese chipmaker direct stakes in leading AI developers, even as its own contract chipmaking business has profited enormously from the AI data center buildout.
The chipmaker’s board approved plans to invest up to $500 million in OpenAI preferred stock reserved for large investors and another $500 million to purchase Anthropic common shares at the latter’s expected IPO price, according to Reuters . Such an investment may give markets “a shot of confidence as the artificial-intelligence trade shows signs of strain,” The Wall Street Journal reported.
But analysts also warned that such circular financing among investors, suppliers, and customers has made it difficult to evaluate real market demand for AI-related hardware and services, according to the Journal.
TSMC’s chip fabrication plants physically produce the vast majority of Nvidia’s chips designed for AI data center workloads and make most of Apple’s custom chips used in iPhones , Mac laptops and desktops, plus Apple’s planned AI computing server . Most of these fabs are located in Taiwan, but TSMC also operates fabs in Japan and China, and it has an expanding US manufacturing cluster in Arizona. It is also building a new fab in Germany.
Meanwhile, Anthropic and OpenAI are both purchasing huge quantities of AI hardware manufactured by TSMC for their data center projects. So TSMC’s announced investment certainly seems to fit into the broader trend of circular financing that has become common during the AI boom, even as tech stocks have gyrated recently as investors have grown increasingly jittery.
Still, TSMC has plenty of money to spare on such a bet. Ongoing demand for AI-related chips has enabled the Taiwanese chipmaker to report six consecutive quarters of record profit, with the latest example being the company earning more than $23 billion in profit during the July through September 2026 quarter, according to Reuters .
TSMC’s planned investment in Anthropic and OpenAI also pales in comparison with circular financing deals from major US tech companies. The most notable example is Nvidia , which has invested $30 billion in OpenAI and $10 billion in Anthropic so far, in addition to putting tens of billions of dollars into many other AI and robotics companies .
