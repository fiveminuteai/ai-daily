How Google Duplex evolved to also call businesses for you in Gemini
Yossi Matias, Vice President, Google & GM, Google Research, Yaniv Leviathan, Google Fellow, Google Research, and Janet Zhou, Group Product Manager, Google Maps
When we introduced Google Duplex in 2018, it pioneered natural voice conversations between AI and local businesses over the phone. Since then, this technology has worked behind the scenes across many Google products. These capabilities are integrated directly in Tasks in Gemini .
Quick links
Completing tasks like making dinner reservations or booking a hair appointment is time consuming. Live phone conversations move fast and rarely follow a script. The person on the other end can say almost anything, often revising their thoughts mid-sentence – and people often rely on subtle tone changes to indicate whose turn it is to speak. Pause half a second too long, and it sounds like the call dropped; speak too soon, and you end up talking over each other. Leveraging AI to complete these tasks respectfully over the phone required us to consider high quality speech understanding, contextual reasoning, and speech synthesis working together in a single, low-latency system.
We introduced Google Duplex nearly a decade ago to help address this challenge. Propelled by advances in foundation models, the Duplex architecture has evolved to power autonomous task completion across many core products, such as Google Search and Maps. This week, we launched Duplex in Tasks in Gemini App , a personal AI collaborator that can help coordinate real-world chores, like calling businesses for you and handling real-world requests on behalf of the user. In this post, we look back at the decade-long architectural evolution that made this possible — moving from early, closed-domain models to a unified stack built entirely with Gemini.
From closed-domain calls to Tasks in Gemini
What began as a narrow, task-specific system quickly expanded into a shared conversational infrastructure, built in collaboration with teams across Google.
During the COVID-19 pandemic, when business hours and services shifted rapidly, Duplex doubled down on verifying merchant info automatically to keep listings accurate. It remains an important data source; for example, business information sourced from Duplex’s phone calls had been shown in Search and Maps over one trillion times , as we shared in 2023.

[S6] OpenAI — "Combatting adversarial distillation" (20 November 2026)
Combatting adversarial distillation
How we’re addressing safety risks of distillation and lessons we’ve learned for strengthening safeguards across the industry.
As frontier AI models become more capable, safeguards play an increasingly important role in how they can be used responsibly. Adversarial distillation creates a path for bad actors to reproduce these capabilities without appropriate safeguards. An actor may use a frontier model’s outputs without authorization to train another model with similar capabilities, then deploy it without comparable protections. For instance, an adversary might distill a model’s cyber capabilities and use the resulting model to help carry out cyberattacks that the original model would have refused to assist with or that its safeguards would have blocked.
We’re sharing how OpenAI detects and disrupts adversarial distillation, how we’ve strengthened protections for our models’ protected reasoning, and what we’ve learned that can help strengthen defenses across the industry.
Our approach to adversarial distillation combines adversarial testing, model training, classifiers, and API-level protections with investigations into coordinated attempts to extract model capabilities. These updated safeguards align more closely with those we use for biology and cybersecurity.
We place particular emphasis on preventing attackers from extracting our models’ protected reasoning: the intermediate steps a model generates internally while working toward an answer, known as its chain-of-thought. These traces can contain information the final answer leaves out, making them especially valuable training data for another model.
W e’ve strengthened our defenses against protected reasoning extraction in GPT‑6.2 Astra and GPT‑6.2 Sol. In an internal adaptive evaluation using strong automated red-teaming , we tested whether attackers could recover protected reasoning the model had generated while answering an earlier prompt. The average attack success rate fell from 54.0% against GPT‑6 Astra with its original safeguards to 0.2% against GPT‑6.2 Astra with its updated safeguards.
Why distillation is a safety issue
Distillation is a common machine-learning technique in which one model learns from data produced by another. For example, a model can learn from another model’s answers, its reasoning, or its judgments about which answers are better and why .
